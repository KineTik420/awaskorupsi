Nadiem Makarim Faces 18-Year Prison Sentence in Chromebook Corruption Case

Former Minister of Education, Culture, Research, and Technology Nadiem Anwar Makarim is facing a prosecution demand of 18 years in prison over alleged corruption in the procurement of Chromebook laptops during his tenure from 2019 to 2024. The demand was read out at the Corruption Crime Court (Tipikor) in Central Jakarta on 13 May 2026.

Background

The Attorney General's Office (Kejaksaan Agung) began investigating the case on 20 May 2025, focusing on the procurement of Chromebook devices for schools under the Ministry of Education's digitisation programme. The total budget involved was approximately Rp 9.9 trillion, allocated between 2020 and 2022 to provide ICT equipment for primary and secondary schools across Indonesia.

How the Case Developed

Investigators initially summoned several members of Nadiem's inner circle — including his special staff Jurist Tan, consultant Ibrahim Arief, and two former ministry directors — before formally naming them as suspects. Nadiem himself was questioned three times as a witness before being named a suspect on 4 September 2025, bringing the total number of suspects to five. Over 120 witnesses were examined during the investigation.

Prosecutors allege that in February 2020, Nadiem met with representatives of Google Indonesia to discuss the Google for Education programme using Chromebook devices — a decision that allegedly steered procurement in favour of a specific vendor. Prosecutors claim state losses amounted to approximately Rp 2.1 trillion.

Charges & Demands

18 years Prison sentence demanded
Rp 1B Fine demanded
Rp 5.68T Total restitution demanded

Prosecutors charged Nadiem under Article 603 of the Corruption Crime Law in conjunction with Article 18 and Article 20(c) of the Criminal Code. In addition to imprisonment and a Rp 1 billion fine, he was demanded to pay restitution of Rp 809.5 billion and Rp 4.87 trillion — totalling approximately Rp 5.68 trillion. If assets are insufficient to cover the restitution, he faces an additional 9 years in prison.

Current Status

Nadiem's detention was converted to house arrest during the trial due to reported health concerns. His legal team disputed the charges, questioning whether the evidence of personal enrichment — which prosecutors linked to an increase in securities holdings — was sufficient. Nadiem himself stated in court that he was confused by the conflicting allegations of receiving cash versus securities.

The trial is ongoing. A verdict has not yet been delivered as of May 2026.
